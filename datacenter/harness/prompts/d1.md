# Stage D1 — as-built check: design layout against site photos

A data hall was fitted out from a design layout, but the installers did not follow the design everywhere. You have the design and survey photos of the hall as it was actually built. Work out what really stands in the hall and deliver the complete as-built equipment layout.

## Inputs (in `inputs/`)

- `design.json` — the **design** layout, in plan millimetres:
  - `grid`, `room.polygon` (inner wall faces of the hall), `columns`, `doors`, `ups_room` (not part of the hall)
  - `racks` and `crah`: each has `id`, `center` (footprint centre), `size` `[width, depth]` and `facing` (compass direction of its front); racks also have their `row` letter
  - `aisles`: the cold aisles as rectangles `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`
- `design_plan.png` — the same design as a drawing.
- `photo_1.jpg` … `photo_7.jpg` — survey photos of the hall **as built**, all taken 3.25 m above the slab:
  - photos 1–5 from five corners of the hall
  - photo 6 from the middle of the south wall
  - photo 7 from the inner corner of the hall next to the UPS room
- `cameras.json` — for each photo:
  - the exact camera position and aim point (metres, plan axes)
  - the lens (mm), the sensor width, and the resolution
  - a ready-made `bench render` command that reproduces that camera in the Blender scene

## What can differ from the design

- The building is as designed: walls, columns, doors, and the raised floor. The floor tiles were laid to the design, so the darker perforated tiles lie in the design's cold aisles even where the racks next to them differ.
- Only the equipment can differ. A rack or a CRAH unit can be missing, be extra, stand in another place, be turned around, or have the other width (600 mm instead of 800 mm, or the reverse).
- The number of differences is not given. Most of the equipment stands exactly as designed.
- The differences are only visible in the photos.

## What you can see in the photos

- **Racks:** black cabinets, 2.0 m high, 1200 mm deep, and 600 mm wide (GPU racks are 800 mm wide). Racks in a row are butted together. The **front** of every rack has a white label strip with a blue LED line under it, across the top of the door. The back has neither.
- **CRAH units:** light-grey cabinets 1800 W × 900 D, with a dark grille on their front (supply side).
- **Floor:** 600 × 600 mm raised-floor tiles on a grid aligned to the plan origin (tile edges at multiples of 600 mm).

## The Blender scene

The scene is already open with:
- the empty hall: `Wall_*`, `Column_*`, and `RaisedFloor` with its top at z = 0.6
- template objects outside the room, each with its origin at the centre of its footprint at floor level and its front facing −y:
  - `RackA` (0.6 × 1.2 × 2.0 m)
  - `RackG` (0.8 × 1.2 × 2.0 m)
  - `CRAH` (1.8 × 0.9 × 1.95 m)
- the seven cameras `Cam_1` … `Cam_7`

You can place copies of the templates (for example, the design layout) and render from the photo cameras to compare with the photos. Your previews are Workbench renders, not photos. The Blender scene is not scored.

## Deliverable: `asbuilt_pred.json` (in the run directory, plan millimetres)

```json
{
  "units": "mm",
  "racks": [{"id": "any string", "center": [x, y], "size": [w, d], "facing": "N|S|E|W"}],
  "crah":  [{"id": "any string", "center": [x, y], "size": [w, d], "facing": "N|S|E|W"}]
}
```

- List **every** rack and **every** CRAH unit that stands in the hall as built, one entry each.
- Equipment that stands as designed must be listed unchanged: copy its `center`, `size` and `facing` from `design.json`.
- `center` is the footprint centre. `size` is `[width, depth]`: width along the row, depth front to back. `facing` is the compass direction the front points to.
- Ids are free-form and are not checked. Entries without a valid `center`, `size` or `facing` are ignored.

## Scoring (100 points)

Racks and CRAH units are scored together as **items**. An item is its centre, its facing and its width (`size[0]`; 1800 for a CRAH unit).

- Two items are **the same** if their centres are at most 150 mm apart, they have the same facing, and their widths differ by at most 50 mm. Each item can be the same as only one other item.
- A **change** is a design item that has no same item in the other layout (an item that is **gone**), or an item of the other layout that has no same item in the design (an item that is **new**). An item that moved, turned or changed width therefore counts as two changes: the old one gone, and a new one.
- The true changes are those between the design and the real as-built layout. Your changes are those between the design and your `asbuilt_pred.json`.
- **70 points — changes.** Each of your changes can match one true change of the same kind (gone / new) whose centre is at most 300 mm away. A new item must also have the same facing and a width within 50 mm of the true new item. The points are 70 × F1, with F1 = 2 × P × R ÷ (P + R), precision P = matched ÷ your changes, and recall R = matched ÷ true changes. Handing in the design unchanged gives 0 here. False changes lower the precision: a design item that you move by more than 150 mm, turn or resize by mistake counts as two wrong changes.
- **30 points — inventory.** Each real as-built item can match one of your items with the same facing and its centre at most 300 mm away. Your items left without a match are surplus. The points are 30 × max(0, matched − surplus) ÷ number of real as-built items.

Finish with `bench submit d1`.
