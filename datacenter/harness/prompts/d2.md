# Stage D2 — design the rack layout of an empty data hall

The data hall is empty. Design the rack layout that fits **as many racks as possible** without breaking any rule of the rule book below. **Exactly 8** of the racks must be GPU racks. There is no layout to copy: a rule checker scores your design.

## Inputs (in `inputs/`)

- `room.json` — the empty hall in plan millimetres (origin at grid A/1, +x east, +y north):
  - `room.polygon`: the **inner wall faces** of the hall (L-shaped). The UPS room is not part of the hall.
  - `columns`: `center` and `size` (600 × 600) of each free-standing column.
  - `doors`: `center` on the wall centreline (the wall is 200 thick, so the inner face is 100 further into the hall), `width`, and `wall` (`S` = the door is in the hall's south wall, `N` = in a north wall).
  - `crah`: the existing cooling units. They are fixed and cannot be moved. `size` is `[width, depth]`, `facing` is the direction the front points to; a unit facing `E` or `W` therefore measures depth along x and width along y.
- `plan_empty.png` — a drawing of the same empty room.
- The Blender scene (`start.blend`, already open): the empty hall (`Wall_*`, `Column_*`, `RaisedFloor` with its top at z = 0.6 m) and template objects `RackA` (0.6 × 1.2 × 2.0 m), `RackG` (0.8 × 1.2 × 2.0 m) and `CRAH` standing outside the room. Each template has its origin at the centre of its footprint at floor level and its front facing −y when unrotated. The fixed CRAH units are **not** placed in the scene; their positions are in `room.json`. The scene is only an aid for looking at your design. **It is not scored**; only the JSON file is.

## Racks

- A rack footprint is `w` × 1200 mm: width `w` along the row, depth 1200 front to back.
- `w` = 600 (standard rack) or 800 (GPU rack). No other sizes.
- Racks are axis-aligned. `facing` is the compass direction the front points to: `N`, `S`, `E` or `W`. A rack facing `N` or `S` measures `w` along x and 1200 along y; a rack facing `E` or `W` measures 1200 along x and `w` along y.

## Rule book (all distances in mm, measured in plan)

- **R1 Inside.** The footprint lies inside the hall polygon.
- **R2 No overlap.** The footprint does not overlap another rack, a column or a CRAH unit. Touching is fine.
- **R3 Front clearance.** The rectangle as wide as the rack that reaches 1200 in front of the front face lies inside the hall and contains no rack, column or CRAH unit.
- **R4 Rear clearance.** The rectangle as wide as the rack that reaches 900 behind the rear face lies inside the hall and contains no rack, column or CRAH unit.
- **R5 Hot/cold separation.** Take the corridor as wide as the rack that reaches 3000 in front of its front face. Every other rack whose footprint overlaps this corridor must face the opposite direction (fronts face fronts).
- **R6 CRAH service zone.** In front of each CRAH unit, the rectangle as wide as the unit (1800) that reaches 1500 from its front face contains no rack.
- **R7 Door zone.** At each door, the rectangle (door width + 600) wide and 1500 deep, centred on the door and starting at the inner wall face, contains no rack.
- **R8 Access.** A person must be able to walk on a path at least 900 wide from door `D1` to the front and to the rear of every rack. The checker does it this way:
  - grid points every 50 mm (at coordinates that are multiples of 50);
  - a grid point is walkable if it is inside the hall and at least 450 from every rack, column, CRAH unit and wall (the walls are solid at the doors too);
  - flood fill over walkable points (4 neighbours) starting from the grid point 600 inside door `D1`;
  - for every rack, the point 600 in front of the centre of its front face and the point 450 behind the centre of its rear face must each be within 150 of a reached grid point.
  - Because of the 50 mm grid, an aisle of exactly 900 only counts if its edges are on multiples of 50. Dead-end aisles are allowed.
- **R9 GPU cooling.** The centre of every GPU rack must be within 7000 of the centre of at least one CRAH unit.

Clearance rectangles of different racks may overlap each other (two rows can share an aisle); they only have to be free of racks, columns and CRAH units.

A rack that breaks any rule is **bad**; all others are **valid**. When two racks conflict under R2, R3, R4 or R5, both are bad. Under R6 and R7 the rack inside the zone is bad.

## Deliverable: `layout_design.json` (in the run directory)

```json
{
  "units": "mm",
  "racks": [
    {"id": "any unique string", "center": [x, y], "size": [600, 1200], "facing": "N"},
    {"id": "any unique string", "center": [x, y], "size": [800, 1200], "facing": "E"}
  ]
}
```

- `center` is the footprint centre in plan millimetres.
- `size` is `[w, 1200]` with `w` = 600 or 800, whatever the facing.
- `facing` is `N`, `S`, `E` or `W`.
- List every rack individually. An entry with missing fields, another size or another facing is counted as a bad rack.

## Scoring

- The checker counts the valid racks, subtracts the bad racks, and subtracts 2 for every GPU rack too many or too few compared with 8 (only valid GPU racks count towards the 8).
- The result is compared with the rack count of a reference design for this room: the score is 100 × (your net count ÷ the reference count), limited to the range 0–100.
- So a bad rack costs more than leaving it out: only hand in racks that pass every rule.

Finish with `bench submit d2`.
