# Stage D3 — route the overhead coolant pipes

A furnished data hall needs six coolant pipes, each running from a wall manifold to the top of one rack. The space under the ceiling is crowded: beams, air ducts, cable trays. Find a route for every pipe that keeps clear of everything, and of the other pipes.

**This stage works in metres only**, in the Blender axes: origin at grid A/1, +x east, +y north, +z up. z = 0 is the top of the structural slab, the raised-floor top is at z = 0.6 and the underside of the ceiling is at z = 3.6.

## Inputs

- The Blender scene is already open (the same file is at `inputs/start.blend`). It holds the furnished hall:
  - **Every mesh object in the scene is an obstacle**, and every obstacle is an axis-aligned box. The object's origin is the box centre and its dimensions are the box size, with no rotation, so `bench inspect` gives each box exactly: `loc` = centre, `dims` = size.
  - The obstacle groups are `Wall_*`, `Column_*`, `RaisedFloor`, `Ceiling`, `Rack_<id>`, `CRAH_*`, `Containment_*`, `CableTray_<row>`, `Beam_*`, `Duct_*` and `Manifold`. Each group has its own viewport colour.
  - Empties `Port_P1` … `Port_P6` and `Target_P1` … `Target_P6` mark the pipe ends. Empties are not obstacles.
  - There are about 110 objects. The `Ceiling` covers the whole hall, so hide it (or look from below it) when you render.
- `inputs/terminals.json`:
  - `ports`: the six manifold outlets `P1` … `P6` (points in front of the `Manifold` box)
  - `targets`: for each pipe, the rack it serves and the target point, which is the centre of that rack's top face
  - `routes`: the assignment — route `Pk` goes from port `Pk` (`from`) to target `Pk` (`to`)
  - `rules`: the numbers used in the rules below
- There is no obstacle list in the JSON: read the obstacles from the Blender scene.

## Rules

A pipe is described by its centreline; its thickness is ignored. A route is valid only if it meets all of C1–C5.

- **C1 — shape.** A route is a list of at least two points `[x, y, z]`. Consecutive points differ in exactly one coordinate, so every segment is parallel to the x, y or z axis (no diagonal segments, no repeated points; coordinates closer than 0.000001 m count as equal). The first point is the route's port and the last point is its target, each within 0.01 m.
- **C2 — clearance.** Every point of the centreline is at least 0.10 m from every obstacle box (the distance from the point to the box; 0 inside the box). The only exception: the parts of the route within 0.30 m path length of its own first point or its own last point are not checked.
- **C3 — height.** z ≤ 3.50 everywhere. z ≥ 2.75 everywhere, except within 0.30 m horizontal distance of the route's own target, where z may go down to 2.60.
- **C4 — bends.** At most 8 bends per route. A bend is a point where the direction changes; a point in the middle of a straight run is not a bend.
- **C5 — separation.** Different routes stay at least 0.15 m apart, centreline to centreline, everywhere along their length. If two routes come closer than that, **both** are invalid, even if one of them already breaks another rule.

The checker samples each route every 0.02 m for C2 and C3 and allows 0.001 m of numerical slack on the limits of C2, C3 and C5.

## Deliverable: `routes.json` (in the run directory, metres)

```json
{
  "units": "m",
  "routes": [
    {"id": "P1", "points": [[x, y, z], [x, y, z], ...]},
    {"id": "P2", "points": [...]}
  ]
}
```

- One entry per pipe, with `id` `P1` … `P6`. `points` runs from the port to the target.
- Only `routes.json` is scored. You may draw the pipes in Blender to check them, but the Blender scene is not scored.

## Scoring

- Each route is scored on its own, and the stage score is the mean of the six routes.
- A missing or invalid route scores 0.
- A valid route scores 60 + 40 × min(1, L_ref / L), where L is the length of your route and L_ref is the length of the shortest valid route for that pipe when the other pipes are ignored. So validity comes first, and shorter valid routes score higher.

Finish with `bench submit d3`.
